Warning Signs to Watch For

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Any gas odor around the heater, which means getting out and calling the gas company before calling us
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater or spreading across the floor
  • Signs of scorching or backdrafting around the base or top of a gas heater
  • Loud popping, banging, or rumbling from the tank that has gotten worse quickly
  • Water at the tap that is suddenly scalding hot
  • A T&P valve that releases water repeatedly instead of staying closed
  • Every tap running cold and the heater showing no signs of life
  • A heater breaker that will not stay on after being reset

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

A water heater emergency packs several hazards into one closet or garage corner. There is water on the floor near a 240-volt circuit on electric models, or near a gas valve, burner, and flue on gas units. There is a tank holding very hot water under pressure. And there is a safety valve that, if it is discharging, has a reason for it. Homeowners sometimes try to stop the relief valve by capping it, tighten a leaking fitting until it cracks, or relight a pilot beside a gas smell, and each of those can turn a bad afternoon into a real disaster. The safest moves for a homeowner are the simple ones: leave for gas, then shut off water and energy, then call for help.

Professional help is not just about having wrenches. It is about knowing why the failure happened so it does not repeat a few weeks later. When a relief valve discharges, we measure static water pressure and look for a missing or failed expansion tank. When a tank floods the floor, we locate the true source, whether it is the valve, a fitting, a connector, or the tank wall. When a gas heater dies, we check the thermocouple or flame sensor, gas pressure, and venting before restarting it. The diagnosis determines everything that follows. Components can be replaced the same visit; a failed tank cannot be patched, and we say so plainly and explain the replacement path.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Tag the water heater supply valve and the main shutoff so nobody has to search during a leak
  • Ask about a drain pan and drain line, especially for heaters in attics, closets, or above finished rooms
  • Store chemicals and flammables far from the burner, and keep the area around the heater uncluttered
  • Drain a few gallons or flush the tank annually to keep sediment from cooking the tank floor
  • Set the temperature around 120F, which protects against burns and slows tank wear
  • A simple water sensor on the floor beside the tank can warn you hours earlier
  • Lift the relief valve lever once a year to confirm it opens and reseats, and call if it keeps dripping
