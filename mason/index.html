How Mason Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a standard tank usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or a damp spot around the base, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new heater with water spreading across the floor.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble needs only a flush, yet that same buildup, ignored through a few more years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and shorten the life of the tank, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number on your phone today so help is one call away the moment something sounds or looks wrong. A quick call saves time, money, and cold showers.

Common Water Heater Problems in Mason

Well before a tank floods a Mason garage, it almost always sends signals. Look out for rumbling and popping during heating, brownish hot water, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, a damp spot under the tank, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. Ignored, they end in a ruptured tank and a cleanup bill. A dripping T&P valve deserves special attention: it's the tank telling you pressure or temperature is running too high, and it should be checked promptly.
